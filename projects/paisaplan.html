01 · The Real Problem

Budgeting apps demand invasive signups and micro-tracking every rupee.

When people start their first job, financial advice is often intimidating. Most budgeting tools immediately ask for phone numbers, OTPs, or bank account syncing before offering any value. Then they expect users to log every single chai and cab fare manually. Within two weeks, people miss entries, feel guilty, and abandon the app. What young earners actually need is high-level allocation clarity before the month even starts, without sacrificing their personal privacy.

02 · The Users

First-time income earners figuring out rent, bills, and savings.

College graduates, early-career professionals, and freelancers receiving their first steady payouts. They want to know: "How much can I spend safely on rent and lifestyle while still setting aside emergency funds?" without needing a finance degree to interpret graphs or worrying about their income data being sold.

03 · The Solution

A zero-login, calm allocation system based on essential priorities.

PaisaPlan strips away 90% of traditional budgeting clutter. Users input their monthly take-home salary and basic fixed commitments. The interface translates that instantly into actionable boundaries using the classic 50% Needs, 30% Wants, and 20% Savings framework. Everything runs purely client-side in the browser—no account creation, no password to remember, and zero data retained.

04 · How It Actually Helps

Financial confidence through upfront visibility, not retrospective guilt.

By planning boundaries before money gets spent, users avoid the anxiety of checking broken budgets at the end of the month. It provides permission to spend on personal joy guilt-free because essential obligations and savings are accounted for first.

Step 01 · Zero Data Footprint

No Login, No Account, Zero Data Saved

Most fintech apps demand phone numbers, OTPs, or bank account syncing before showing any value. For young earners, this creates immediate privacy friction and fear of financial spam.

PaisaPlan requires no login, no database, and saves no personal data. You type your numbers, see your allocation immediately in the browser, and when you close the tab, everything vanishes cleanly.

Step 02 · Clarity

Output: Clean Boundaries

The summary screen immediately translates figures into three readable buckets: Needs (50%), Wants (30%), and Savings & Investments (20%).

High-contrast color blocks emphasize what is safely spendable each week, preventing end-of-month surprises and keeping users mentally relaxed.

Reflection

What This Project Taught Me

Personal finance interfaces frequently confuse information density with usefulness. For someone entering the workforce, seeing twenty customizable categories causes analysis paralysis.

Designing PaisaPlan proved that giving users fewer, well-framed choices—backed by true privacy and zero friction—creates genuine financial confidence and sustained habits.
