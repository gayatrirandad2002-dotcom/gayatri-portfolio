PriceGate

A B2B mobile app that helps small Indian industrial businesses share daily commodity prices with customers — with full access control, owner-only price history, and zero manual WhatsApp work.

Context Indian Industrial SME
Research Real Business Owner Workflow
Core Architecture Two-Tier Asymmetric Visibility
Role Product & UX Designer

01 / The Problem

How commodity businesses actually operate.

A small industrial business owner from Maharashtra.

  • Every morning: manually sending updated product prices to customers on WhatsApp.
  • Price history: sitting trapped in an Excel sheet.
  • Business partners: calling him directly whenever they need the same rate info.

This is how thousands of small commodity businesses across India actually work. Not because they want to, but because nothing better existed for them.

02 / The Insight That Changed Everything

The owner and customer need completely different things from the same app.

Owner Needs

Full price history, 30-day trends, and total control over who sees what.

Customer Needs

Just today's price. Nothing more.

Giving both users the same interface would be wrong. So I didn't.

03 / The Solution

Two Worlds. One App.

Customer sees today only:

  • Clean price list: Only items whose prices change daily.
  • Timestamped certainty: Date and time stamped with a live status indicator.
  • Strict privacy: No price history, no other customers' data, nothing extra.
  • Lock notice: Clear, honest transparency about what is visible and what is private.

Owner sees everything:

  • Today's rates: Clear up/down change indicators with day deltas.
  • Full price history: Every product, every date, every price movement.
  • Access control: Approve or reject customer access requests instantly.
  • Multi-device sync: Up to 3 partner devices logged in simultaneously.
  • Product lifecycle: Add, edit, or archive products without losing past data.

04 / Key Design Decisions

1. Two-tier visibility

Owner sees all. Customer sees today. This is not a limitation — it is the product. The owner's price history is competitive information. Giving it to customers removes their business advantage. The asymmetry is intentional and it is the most important decision in the entire app.

2. Instagram-style access control

Customers send a request. Owner approves or rejects — exactly like a follow request. Every business owner and customer already understands this flow. Zero learning curve for a completely new system.

3. Multi-device owner access

Most small Indian businesses have 2–3 partners. All need the same information at the same time. The app allows up to 3 devices on the owner side simultaneously — designed for how these businesses actually work.

4. Light professional visual design

Not a stock market terminal. Not a consumer lifestyle app. Something in between — clean, trustworthy, fast to read. Navy for authority. Amber gold for price numbers — because commodity prices in Indian industrial context carry that visual weight naturally. Green and red for up and down — universally understood. Nothing random.

5. Archived products — not deleted

When a product is no longer sold, it gets archived and hidden from customers. But history is preserved for the owner because past price data retains business value even after a product is discontinued.

6. Daily price update flow

The owner opens the app every morning, updates today's prices for all products in one place, and customers see the update immediately. What used to be 10 WhatsApp messages every morning becomes one tap.

05 / Research Behind This

This project started from a real conversation — not a made-up persona.

One honest conversation with an actual industrial business owner gave me more insight than any formal research plan could. Because I asked about his real daily workflow, his specific frustrations, and exactly what he wished existed.

That conversation gave me the core insight — two users, two completely different needs, one app that serves both without confusion.

06 / What I Learned

The hardest design decision was not what to add. It was what to keep separate.

Most apps try to give everyone everything. PriceGate is built on the opposite principle — give each user only exactly what they need. Not more. Not less.

"Designing restraint into a system is harder than designing features into it."
